Dense CLIP Localization Gains Do Not Consistently Transfer to
Relational Object Selection
Authors and affiliations to be determined
Abstract
We report a separation between category-sensitive local-
ization and relational object selection in evaluated CLIP
pipelines. A map that highlights a category need not iden-
tify the intended instance among objects of that category. We
analyze saved COCO experiments with a frozen ViT-B/16, or-
dinary and modified final-block readouts, and released Dense
Cosine Similarity Map (DCSM) scorers.
At 224-pixel in-
put resolution, a ClearCLIP-style readout increases proposal-
based detection AP from 2.6 to 27.3 on 4,952 images, yet its
mean-map scorer obtains 39.4% balanced spatial-selection ac-
curacy, versus 50.3% for the ordinary readout. On 527 spatial
queries from 375 images, released DCSM ObjA and COCO
scorers reach 58.1% [53.7, 62.4] and 57.3% [53.2, 61.2], re-
spectively, against 45.7% expected chance (exploratory per-
centile 95% image-cluster bootstrap intervals). These condi-
tional, annotation-derived results motivate separate validation
of localization and relational selection; they neither isolate a
causal mechanism nor test learned DCSM scoring on modi-
fied features.
1
Introduction and Related Work
CLIP compares image and text representations learned
through natural-language supervision [7]. Applying these rep-
resentations to objects involves two questions: whether a cat-
egory produces a useful spatial response, and whether a de-
scription selects the intended instance. A heatmap can high-
light every person while failing to select the person to the
left of a bicycle. This distinction matters when dense-feature
improvements are used to motivate stronger object-level lan-
guage understanding.
Dense feature extraction and dense image–text scoring ad-
dress different parts of this problem.
MaskCLIP extracts
dense predictions from pretrained CLIP [8]. ClearCLIP de-
composes the final block, removing residual and feed-forward
paths and using self-self attention to improve segmentation
[3]. DCSM instead retains token–patch similarities and ap-
plies a learned convolutional scorer [2]. Their original tasks
and designs are not interchangeable: improvements in seg-
mentation do not directly measure relation-conditioned in-
stance selection. We evaluate readouts inspired by MaskCLIP
and ClearCLIP, not full reproductions of their segmentation
results.
Detection provides a second reference point.
OWL-ViT
adapts image–text pretraining through detection fine-tuning
[5], and OWLv2 scales detection supervision through self-
training [6]. Their learned box and score mechanisms differ
from frozen-CLIP map pooling. Moreover, fine-grained de-
tector evaluation has precedent in FG-OVD [1]. We therefore
do not claim a new detector, a first compositional benchmark,
or established novelty over that literature.
Our focused observation is that localization and detection
gains from the evaluated CLIP readouts do not consistently
transfer to relational candidate selection. We connect three
existing measurements: oracle-category pointing, category-
conditioned detection, and selection among supplied same-
category boxes. The main comparison uses spatial descrip-
tions; size and attachment diagnostics constrain its interpreta-
tion. The study contributes:
• A cross-task comparison separating category localiza-
tion from relational selection.
• A protocol and construction audit bounding the observed
transfer differences.
The evidence is a saved-artifact analysis, not newly rerun
inference. Its practical implication is an evaluation require-
ment: validate the downstream selection rule independently,
rather than infer relational capability from category heatmaps
or detection AP.

2.2
Localization and detection
The oracle-category probe uses the first 500 eligible COCO
val2017 images in sorted ID order.
A trial is an image–
category pair: pointing succeeds if the maximum patch cen-
ter lies in any non-crowd box of the known category. It does
not ask which same-category instance a relational descrip-
tion intends. Pointing is micro-averaged over eligible image–
category trials, not images; the trial count was not retained in
the summary. The non-random probe does not support image-
cluster intervals from the retained aggregates.
Detection covers 4,952 images with non-crowd annotations.
We report COCO bbox AP averaged over IoU thresholds 0.50–
0.95 on a 0–100 scale. Class prompts are a photo of a
{category}. for the 80 COCO categories [4]. Two proto-
cols connect the same maps to different box mechanisms:
P1, dense components. Bilinearly upsample maps to the
input square (224 or 448 pixels, align_corners=False),
apply an 80-way class softmax with multiplier 100, retain
winning-class pixels with probability above 0.3, and extract
four-connected components of at least four upsampled pixels.
Maximum component probability is the box score.
P2, supplied proposals. Select the top 100 OWLv2 boxes
by sigmoid objectness. Average class maps over patch cen-
ters inside each box, using the patch containing the box center,
clamped to the grid, for empty memberships. Apply the same
softmax multiplier, retain three classes per box, and multiply
each class probability by objectness. Readout variants share
proposals and scoring rules within P2.
Native OWL references instead retain the top 300 box–
class pairs by sigmoid class logits across predicted boxes.
Native OWLv2 and P2 therefore do not have identical final
proposals or confidence rules.
An AP difference between
them is not a pure classification comparison.
Reference
models are google/owlvit-base-patch32 and google/
owlv2-base-patch16-ensemble; their training and back-
bones are also unmatched to frozen CLIP.
2.3
Conditional relational selection
Spatial items derive from COCO boxes. Each query supplies
two to six same-category candidates 𝐴and a single annotated
reference instance 𝐵. One target lies on the requested side of
𝐵, with a center margin of 0.05 of the relevant image dimen-
sion and separation constraints; all distractor centers lie on
the opposite side. Relations are left, right, above, and below
in the image plane, not three-dimensional judgments. All non-
crowd target-category instances must have annotation-area ra-
tio at least 0.002 and box side lengths at least 24 pixels. Gen-
eration uses seed 0 and per-subset caps of 60 items per cat-
egory and two per image. The spatial subset contains 527
queries from 375 images and 57 target categories. Its two box-
area strata contain 233 target-largest and 294 target-not-largest
items.
Whole-image map scorers average query similarities over
patches inside each supplied candidate box at 224 resolution,
using nearest-patch fallback when necessary, without a class
softmax or learned CNN. CLIP crop scoring compares the
crop CLS embedding with query EOS. Candidate crops add
10% padding per side; spatial context crops use the union of
the candidate and the reference box. These context crops use
privileged annotations and can change object scale and layout.
The task is consequently conditional candidate selection, not
end-to-end referring-expression detection.
Two released DCSM checkpoints [2], locally called ObjA
and COCO, score context crops using ordinary CLIP features
and an already trained CNN. The adapter supplies an image-
position-by-text-position matrix (197-by-30), including im-
age CLS and text BOS/EOS/padding positions, with two con-
volutional layers and a 128-unit hidden fully connected layer.
It inserts checkpoint functional embeddings using inherited
whitespace positions and standardizes over all entries of the
candidate-by-channel-by-image-position-by-text-position ten-
sor for a query. Tokenizer alignment is not independently cer-
tified; normalization makes scores candidate-batch dependent.
No new training is performed here, but these scorers are not
training-free components. Modified readouts with the learned
CNN were not evaluated. Local filenames and implementa-
tion provenance are recorded in the non-public supplement;
checkpoint hashes and release versions remain unavailable.
OWL candidate scoring matches predicted boxes to each
supplied candidate at IoU at least 0.5 and takes the maximum
matching query logit. If no prediction matches, the best-IoU
prediction supplies its logit minus 10. OWL selection thus in-
cludes localization and matching effects, unlike directly scor-
ing supplied crops.

Results
3.1
Category-localization and proposal gains
Table 1 tests whether readout changes improve category-
sensitive responses. At 224 pixels, pooled pointing rises from
12.0% to 62.4% with the ClearCLIP-style output, while P2
AP rises from 2.6 to 27.3. The shared P2 protocol supports
a readout effect in this detector, but AP still mixes box qual-
ity, calibration, and ranking. In P1, the same ClearCLIP-style
pooled configuration obtains only 3.0 AP. The highest P1 AP
across the full evaluated grid rounds to 4.6, so this component
rule does not convert useful maps into a strong detector.
Higher
resolution
does
not
ensure
detection
gains:
ClearCLIP-style pooled pointing reaches 67.5% at 448 pixels,
while P2 AP becomes 26.4. Native OWLv2 reaches 46.8 AP
under its separate protocol. Neither comparison licenses the
claim that localization alone determines detection or that the
native/P2 gap measures classification alone.

3.3
Construction diagnostics constrain the in-
terpretation
The retained collection also contains size, color, without, and
with queries; the detailed manuscript reports all 1,833 items
and 12 scorers. We retain two diagnostics because they ex-
plain why selection accuracy alone can be misleading. In size
queries, big descriptions outnumber small descriptions 575
to 80. The largest-box heuristic reaches 87.3% raw accuracy
without reading language; OWLv2 falls from 64.7% raw to
41.7% after big/small reweighting. Size labels use annotation
area rather than box area, so even a balanced box heuristic is
not guaranteed to equal 50%.
Attachment-based without/with queries are not matched
controls. Their builder prioritizes without when both rules
are satisfied; two-candidate items account for 273/315 with-
out items but only 44/194 with items. Overlap or center con-
tainment proxies attachment, and missing annotations do not
prove visual absence. DCSM without accuracies are 38.7%
and 37.5% against 48.1% chance, but ordinary token-max
reaches 55.7%. Thus neither universal negation failure nor a
causal effect of changing polarity follows from this collection.

4
Discussion and Limitations
A category map and a relational selection rule require sepa-
rate validation. One possible explanation is that scalar aggre-
gation loses interactions useful for selection, whereas learned
matrix scoring retains them. The experiments do not isolate
that mechanism: DCSM uses different crops and prior super-
vision, and spatial context can change the relative scale of tar-
get and reference. The observation is therefore a cross-task
diagnostic, not proof that final-block surgery harms learned
DCSM scoring or that topology alone explains its advantage.
The spatial labels are filtered, templated, image-plane judg-
ments.
There are 404 two-candidate items, and left/right
queries outnumber above/below queries. Area reweighting
does not remove these imbalances. Human verification of tar-
get uniqueness and label quality is incomplete. Ground-truth
candidates and reference boxes simplify the problem; unseen-
category generalization and independent referring-expression
evaluation are not measured. Reliable deployment would need
candidate discovery and robustness checks beyond these anno-
tations.
Training/evaluation overlap, exact checkpoint hashes, and
original execution revisions remain incompletely audited.
Frozen evaluation does not imply no prior training. Retained
summaries and predictions support arithmetic checks, not a
newly reproduced inference run. Original hardware and com-
plete package inventories are unavailable, and no matched
speed claim is made. Bootstrap intervals capture sample vari-
ability only; they cannot repair proxy labels or certify gener-
alization.
A controlled follow-up would match crop geometry and su-
pervision across readouts and learned scorers, use paired pos-
itive/negative descriptions, and validate labels on an indepen-
dent collection. Those studies are not part of this report.
5
Conclusion
The evaluated CLIP readouts improve category localization
and proposal-based detection without consistently improving
relational candidate selection. Released DCSM scorers have a
spatial advantage on this constructed sample, but unmatched
inputs and training prevent causal attribution. Separate lo-
calization, detection, and selection measurements expose this
boundary without overstating universal reasoning limitations.
